# HelpPuff logo brief

## Decision (2026-10-12)

The logo is **Puff-tail p**, from a second round that combined *Bubble p*
(concept 9) with *Rising Puffs* (concept 5): a lowercase "p" whose bowl is a
round speech bubble, with a short speech tail that ends in one small puff.
Half speech bubble, half breath. The other concepts' files were removed;
this brief is kept as the record of what was considered.

Colours (2026-10-13): the Oxblood & Electric Citron palette, as on the
website: oxblood `#3B0D24` on light backgrounds, citron `#E4F568` on dark
ones, and citron on an oxblood tile where one image must work on both.

Files here: `helppuff.svg` (the mark, one path, oxblood), `helppuff-dark.svg` (citron),
`helppuff-icon.svg` (citron on a rounded oxblood tile),
`helppuff-mono.svg` (`currentColor`), `helppuff-tile.svg` (white on indigo,
for app icons and avatars), `helppuff-lockup.svg` and
`helppuff-lockup-dark.svg` (with the wordmark, Plus Jakarta Sans Bold as
paths), `favicon.ico` (16, 32, 48) and `png/` (sizes, the tile, the lockups
and `social-preview.png` for GitHub's Settings → Social preview).

## Where we start

The current mark (`website/public/logo.svg`) is a speech bubble with three
"typing" dots and an indigo-to-violet gradient. It is friendly, but it is the
default picture for "chat": iMessage, every helpdesk widget and most AI
assistants use some form of it. It has nothing that says *puff*, *yours*
or *small*. Its gradient also turns muddy at 16 px and has no
single-colour form.

What HelpPuff is: an MIT-licensed AI chat assistant for websites that runs in
the owner's own Cloudflare account on the free plan, with a tiny widget
(around 6 KB for the loader) and no vendor in between. The brand colour
is `#5B5BF7` (indigo).

## 1. Brand traits

1. **Light**: soft, airy and small. It should feel weightless, like a puff
   of air or a 6 KB script, never heavy or corporate.
2. **Friendly but competent**: warm enough for a small business's website,
   serious enough that a developer would trust it with their customers.
3. **Owned**: it belongs to the site owner. Self-contained, with no vendor
   in the middle. It should say "your own assistant", not "a platform".
4. **Open**: MIT, transparent and plain. Nothing hidden, nothing
   ornamental.
5. **Talks**: it should still read as conversation, so people recognise a
   chat assistant at a glance.

## Must avoid

1. **AI clichés**: robot heads, antennae, circuit traces, sparkle/✨ stars,
   brains. They date fast and say "AI vendor", which is the opposite of
   "owned by you".
2. **Lookalikes of chat-widget brands**: Intercom (rounded square with a
   "smile" of vertical bars), Crisp (bubble with a face), Drift, Tidio,
   Messenger (bubble with a lightning bolt), iMessage (plain bubble). Also
   avoid **Cloudflare's orange cloud** and iCloud's outline cloud: we sit
   on Cloudflare and must not look like its own product.
3. **Detail that dies small**: gradients, thin strokes, inner dots under
   about 4 units, text inside the mark. It has to hold up as a 16 px
   favicon and in one colour (embroidery, a stamp, a monochrome README
   badge).

## 2. Concepts

Twelve concepts in six directions.

### Chat bubble

**1. Cloud Bubble**
A speech bubble whose top edge is three soft cloud puffs, with a normal
speech tail underneath.
*Why:* it says "puff" and "talk" in a single silhouette, with no detail to
lose.
*Risk:* a scalloped edge is the comic-book sign for a *thought* bubble, and
cloud-plus-chat marks already exist.

**2. Breath Tail**
A plain round bubble whose tail curls away like a wisp of breath, not a
sharp point.
*Why:* the only change from a standard bubble is the "puff", so it is subtle
and ownable.
*Risk:* the curl is the one thing that makes it distinct, and it shrinks to
a blob at 16 px.

**3. Open Bubble**
A bubble drawn as a thick ring with a gap where the tail would be: an open
conversation.
*Why:* a nod to open source; light and airy.
*Risk:* looks like a loading spinner or a "C"; weak as a filled favicon.

**4. Keyhole Bubble**
A solid round bubble with a keyhole cut out of it. The keyhole doubles as
a small person (head and shoulders).
*Why:* "owned by you" in one picture: the conversation is yours, and so is
the person in it.
*Risk:* it can read as a password manager or secure messenger rather than a
friendly helper.

### Cloud / puff

**5. Rising Puffs**
Three circles that grow as they rise from the lower left, like puffs of
breath or a voice getting louder.
*Why:* the purest "puff": light and minimal, and it could animate as the
widget's typing indicator.
*Risk:* abstract dots are hard to own and can look like bubbles in water or a
"loading" mark.

**6. Pillow Cloud**
A three-lobed cloud with a flat base and a small speech bubble cut out of
its middle.
*Why:* a soft cloud (also a nod to Cloudflare) holding the conversation.
*Risk:* the closest of all to Cloudflare or iCloud, and the inner bubble is
small.

### Mascot / character

**7. Puffy**
A round little character with puffed-out cheeks, as if holding its breath,
and two dot eyes. No mouth and no robot parts.
*Why:* a character makes the brand warm and memorable, and the puffed cheeks
are the name made literal.
*Risk:* mascots drift toward Crisp's face-in-a-bubble, and cuteness can
undercut "trust it with your customers".

**8. Little Ghost of a Breath**
A teardrop of air with two eyes, drifting sideways.
*Why:* soft, light and characterful.
*Risk:* reads as a ghost (Snapchat) or a water drop (a utility company).

### Letterform

**9. Bubble p**
A lowercase "p" whose bowl is a round speech bubble and whose stem ends in a
pointed speech tail.
*Why:* the initial and the product in one shape, and very solid at small
sizes.
*Risk:* "letter inside a bubble" is a common pattern; it needs a distinctive
cut to be ownable.

**10. Puff H**
A capital "H" whose crossbar is a small cloud puff.
*Why:* a monogram that starts the name, with the puff as the connecting idea.
*Risk:* three ideas in one small square; "H" monograms are everywhere, and
HP evokes Hewlett-Packard.

### Abstract / geometric

**11. Exhale**
A soft squircle with three horizontal swooshes leaving its side, like a
gust of breath.
*Why:* motion and lightness; sounds like "puff".
*Risk:* looks like a weather or "wind" icon, or a fast-delivery logo.

### Ownership / place

**12. Home Bubble**
A speech bubble whose top rises to a gentle roof peak: a conversation that
lives at home.
*Why:* "self-hosted, owned by you" as a picture.
*Risk:* reads as real estate or a smart-home app; the peak fights the
softness.

### The five to build

They were picked by score, but also so that no two say the same thing in the
same way:

| Build | Concept | Direction | What it leans on |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| concept-1 | Cloud Bubble | chat bubble + cloud | the name ("puff") |
| concept-2 | Puffy | mascot | warmth and memorability |
| concept-3 | Keyhole Bubble | ownership | "owned by you" |
| concept-4 | Rising Puffs | abstract | lightness and "tiny" |
| concept-5 | Bubble p | letterform | the initial, sturdiness |

Breath Tail (13) scored close, but it is a quieter version of Cloud Bubble
(a bubble plus one puff detail), so Rising Puffs takes the slot because it
is a genuinely different idea.

### Worth combining later

- **Cloud Bubble + Bubble p**: a "p" whose bowl has the cloud's scalloped
  top.
- **Puffy + Cloud Bubble**: give the cloud bubble Puffy's two eyes for a
  mascot version of the main mark (for the widget's launcher and avatar).
- **Rising Puffs as motion**: whichever mark wins, the three growing puffs
  could become the widget's typing indicator, replacing the generic three
  dots.
- **Keyhole as a secondary mark**: the keyhole-person on its own could
  stand for "your data" in docs and the Security page.
